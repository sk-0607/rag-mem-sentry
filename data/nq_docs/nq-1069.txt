Sharks are usually seen to live a solitary existence, rarely moving about in group events, although, a tank could house up to four or five species during the same time period[6]. It has been commonly seen that Lemon and Nurse sharks occupy the bottom of the tank floor. Occasionally, they will take a swim no more than two or three feet off the bottom[7]. Bull sharks, Sandbar sharks, and Sand tiger sharks continuously swim at mid-depth. Larger tiger sharks inhabit the upper region of the tank where their dorsal fin is breaking the surface frequently[8]. Swimming patterns seen from sharks in captivity are that of Blacktip, Bull, and Lemon sharks being active 24 hours and those of Sandbars, Nurse and Sand tigers being active at certain times of the day/night[9].

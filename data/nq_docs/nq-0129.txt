The origins of the interchange fee are a matter of some controversy.  Often they are assumed to have been developed to maintain and attract a proper mix of issuers and acquirers to bank networks.  Research by Professor Adam Levitin of Georgetown University Law Center, however, indicates that interchange fees were originally designed as a method for banks to avoid usury and Truth-in-Lending laws.[9]  Typically, the bulk of the fee goes to the issuing bank.

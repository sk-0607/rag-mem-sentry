During World War I, the Pale lost its rigid hold on the Jewish population when large numbers of Jews fled into the Russian interior to escape the invading German army.[citation needed] On March 20 (April 2 N.S.), 1917, the Pale was abolished by the Provisional Government decree, On abolition of confessional and national restrictions (Russian: Об отмене вероисповедных и национальных ограничений).[citation needed]  A large portion of the Pale, together with its Jewish population, became part of Poland.[citation needed] Subsequently, most of this population would perish in The Holocaust one generation later.

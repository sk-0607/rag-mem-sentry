The bridge's upper level originally contained two pedestrian walkways and two elevated railway tracks (which connected a spur of the IRT Second Avenue Elevated Line in Manhattan to the Queensboro Plaza station in Queens).[10] Three lanes of roadway were installed on the south side of the upper level in 1931, replacing the former upper-level walkway.[14] All service on the Second Avenue Elevated was discontinued in 1942.[15] From 1955 to 1958, two additional lanes were built on the upper level. The upper-level ramps on the Queens end of the bridge were built during the same time.[16]

The three paleontologists Xiaoming Wang, Richard H. Tedford, and Ronald M. Nowak have proposed that C. dirus evolved from Canis armbrusteri,[19]:52[20]:181 with Nowak stating that specimens found in Cumberland Cave, Maryland, appear to be C. armbrusteri diverging into C. dirus.[28][29]:243 The early wolf from China, Canis chihliensis, may have been the ancestor of both C. armbrusteri and the gray wolf C. lupus.[20]:146 The sudden appearance of C. armbrusteri in North America during the Early Pleistocene suggests that this was an immigrant from Asia,[20]:144 as was C. lupus later in the Pleistocene.[20]:144[29]:242 In 2010 Francisco Prevosti proposed that C. dirus was a sister taxon to C. lupus.[30]:472

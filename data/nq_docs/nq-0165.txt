Bosley Crowther of The New York Times said, inter alia: "The minor musical whimsey that arrived at the Palace and other theaters yesterday under the just-too-cunning title of “Robin and the 7 Hoods” is almost as strained and archaic in the fable it has to tell of Prohibition-era gangsters in Chicago as the fable of Robin Hood it travesties. Dishing up Frank Sinatra as the leader of a mob that enhances its public image by giving large sums of money to charity, it runs through some all-too-familiar plot arrangements and farce routines that have a fleeting and far-away resemblance to some of the stuff in the old Damon Runyon tales."[6]
